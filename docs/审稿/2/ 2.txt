
Review of "Analysis of Out-of-Band Emissions in Sub-Band Full Duplex 5G-NTN operating at FR1"

Paper ID: 331-31543

---

General Comments

This paper asks whether Sub-band Full Duplex (SBFD) is feasible for LEO-based 5G Non-Terrestrial Networks in the FR1 TDD band. The motivation is clear: conventional TDD wastes 60–95% of frame resources on guard periods in NTN due to long propagation delays, and SBFD reframes this as a frequency-domain interference problem. The authors build a two-satellite system model using TR 38.811 antenna patterns, 3GPP spectrum emission masks, and free-space path loss, then report downlink and uplink SINR and throughput as a function of guard-band size and UE position.

SBFD is under active 3GPP standardization, and applying it to NTN is a legitimate open question. But the paper has four problems that prevent a confident feasibility conclusion. First, the scenario is sparse by construction — two satellites, one UE per beam, no fading — yet the conclusions are stated broadly. Second, finding that OOB interference falls well below the noise floor is not surprising given free-space path losses at LEO ranges and a single-interferer geometry; the result needs a harder stress test to be interesting. Third, there is no comparison against conventional TDD in spectral efficiency or latency, so the claimed SBFD advantage is asserted rather than demonstrated. Fourth, key reproducibility details are missing: UE–UE distance, beam separation angle, and specific SEM/BEM values are not tabulated.

For these reasons, I recommend the paper accepted with major revisions .

Specific Comments

PP 1, Par 1 (Abstract): The abstract states that "UL→DL leakage can become dominant in worst-case UE proximity," but no simulation of this scenario appears in the paper. Either add the simulation or remove this claim.

PP 1, Par 4 (Introduction): The "4× cell-edge UL throughput and 32% average UL throughput" figures come from [8], a terrestrial study. The current phrasing implies these hold for NTN; they do not. Clarify this.

PP 2, Par 1: The paper promises to evaluate "overall spectral efficiency," but Table IV reports throughput in Mbit/s, not bits/s/Hz. The promised metric is never computed. Correct this or change the stated objective.

PP 2, Introduction  "SBFD presents a viable pathway for the efficient coexistence of terrestrial and non-terrestrial networks" — but the paper models two NTN satellites, not terrestrial-NTN coexistence. This sentence is unsupported by the results and should be revised or removed.

PP 2, Section II-A: The "60–95% frame overhead" claim drives the entire motivation. Provide a brief derivation or worked numerical example for a representative LEO orbit and frame configuration rather than relying on citations alone.

PP 3, Table II: The table header says "for NTN Systems," but the figures in the table ("4× cell-edge, 32% average," "5.41–6.92 dB") come from terrestrial evaluations in [7] and [8]. Annotate them accordingly.

PP 3, Section III (second paragraph): Two satellites and one UE per beam is the sparsest plausible deployment — interference is minimized by construction. Discuss how results change under realistic multi-beam, multi-UE conditions, or at least acknowledge this as a bounding case.

PP 3, Eq. (1)–(2): Free-space path loss is a clean starting point, but the authors' own framing ("worst case for received interference") cuts both ways: the desired signal is also at its strongest, so the noise-limited conclusion may be optimistic once fading, scintillation, or clutter is introduced. Acknowledge this asymmetry explicitly. See ITU-R P.681 and 3GPP TR 38.811 for channel models that could inform a follow-up.

PP 3, Section III-A: The UL bandwidth is fixed at 10 MHz per beam with no justification. SBFD's main degree of freedom is flexible UL/DL partitioning. A sensitivity sweep over BUL would make this section substantively useful.

PP 4, Eq. (5): The Bessel reflector model uses a = 10λ, following TR 38.811, but the corresponding antenna diameter and peak gain are never stated. These values enter every link budget calculation and should appear in Table III.

PP 4, Section III-D.1, Eq. (13)–(14): UE-to-UE path loss LUE−UE is critical for the UL→DL interference term, yet Table III contains no assumed UE–UE distance. The results cannot be reproduced. State the assumed distance (or range of distances) explicitly.

PP 5, Eq. (19): IDL→UL ≈ 0 is reasonable for the inter-satellite path, but in a multi-beam scenario from the same satellite, DL→UL coupling through sidelobes may not be negligible. The assumption silently removes an entire interference dimension; it deserves a sentence of justification rather than a passing reference to antenna pointing.

PP 5, Table III: Several parameters needed for reproducibility are missing: beam separation angle between Satellite 1 and Satellite 2, UE–UE distance, the specific SEM values from TS 38.101-1, and the BEM values from ECC Report 281 (with table/figure numbers). Add these.

PP 6, Fig. 6: The SINR curves overlapping across all guard-band values is the central result, but it is shown for one beam geometry. Beam separation controls cross-beam coupling, and the paper never explores this dimension. A parametric study on angular separation would turn an observation into a finding.

PP 6, Fig. 7: I/N ratios appear only for GB = 5 MHz. Since the main argument is that guard-band size does not matter, showing I/N for all four GB values is necessary for internal consistency.

PP 6, Section IV-B: UL→UL is identified as the dominant interference source, but no mitigation strategies are discussed. For a paper arguing SBFD is viable, the most consequential interference term deserves more than a diagnosis — power control, beam scheduling, or frequency hopping are all candidates worth at least a paragraph.

PP 7, Table IV: UL throughput at beam edge is 0.101 Mbit/s across all configurations. Is this useful for any intended NTN service? More importantly, without a TDD baseline with appropriate guard periods, there is no way to tell whether SBFD is actually better. This comparison is the crux of the paper and is entirely absent.
