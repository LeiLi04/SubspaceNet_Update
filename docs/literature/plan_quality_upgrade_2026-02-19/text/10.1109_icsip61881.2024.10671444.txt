Direction of Arrival Tracking using Orthogonal
Dipoles
Miao Wang
Dept. of Data Management
Postal Savings Bank of China
Beijing, China
Abstract—In this paper, we propose a novel approach for
tracking the direction of arrival (DOA) using a collocated
microphone array equipped with double dipole microphones.
Our method consists of two main steps. First, we employ a
DOA estimation procedure based on a histogram-based wrapped
Gaussian mixture model (Hist-WGMM). Second, we introduce a
new Kalman filter based on the wrapped Gaussian distribution.
The DOA estimation procedure is designed for real-time deploy-
ment and exhibits robustness against reverberation and noise.
Additionally, our proposed Kalman filter mitigates the issue of
oscillations around 0◦, a common problem faced by conventional
Kalman filters. Through numerical simulations, we validate the
effectiveness of our entire tracking method, demonstrating its
ability to accurately estimate and track DOA in a fast and precise
manner.
Index Terms—DOA estimation, DOA tracking, orthogonal
dipoles, Kalman filter, mixture model
I. INTRODUCTION
Differential microphone arrays (DMAs) are extensively em-
ployed in speech processing. In [1], [2], an adaptive DMA was
devised to mitigate the interference of undesired speeches on
the desired speech signal. Furthermore, DMAs are utilized to
estimate the power spectral density (PSD) of ambient noise
and improve speech quality by subtracting noisy spectra [3],
[4]. Theoretical investigations have been conducted on the
design and implementation of linear DMAs [5]. Over the
past decade, a variant of DMA, the circular differential mi-
crophone array (CDMA), has garnered increased attention in
audio processing. A general approach to designing frequency-
invariant beam patterns such as dipoles and cardioids has
been developed using uniform CDMAs in [6]. Specifically,
a uniform CDMA comprising 4 omni microphones distributed
evenly on a circle with a radius of 2cm has been employed to
create two orthogonal dipole beam patterns [7], [8]. Based on
these two dipoles, a DOA estimation method utilizing error
reduction functions, denoted as DOA-IOD, has been proposed
to locate the speaker [9].
However, the aforementioned uniform CDMA with a diam-
eter of 4cm still requires a relatively large space for some edge
devices like wearable sensors. Moreover, the relative position
between sound sources and microphones usually changes in
practice. Additionally, the aforementioned work [9] does not
address the issues of tracking DOA estimates. On the other
hand, benefiting from the development of microelectrome-

III. PROPOSED DOA TRACKING METHOD
In the context of Bayesian filters, the DOA estimates
obtained in section II can be regarded as observations. This
implies that the tracking procedures like the Kalman filter [16]
can be applied to these observations to achieve better tracking
results. However, due to the phase ambiguity introduced in
Eq.(3), the assumption based on Gaussian distributions of
conventional Kalman filter does not hold any more. Instead,
the wrapped Kalman filter (WKF) [17] is the appropriate
choice for tracking DOAs. In this section, we extend the theory
of WKF, which can solve phase ambiguity between 0 and
2π during tracking, and generalize it to be feasible in phase
ambiguity between 0 and T. We fix T as π in this paper.

IV. THE ENTIRE PROCEDURE
We’ve consolidated all components of the proposed DOA
tracking method into a diagram, depicted in Fig.3. Firstly,
the bandpass filter B(f, p) is applied to the time-frequency
representations, X(t, f) and Y (t, f), resulting in EX(t, p)
and EY (t, p) respectively. Next, the threshold value ET (p),
derived from microphone calibration, is applied to EX(t, p)
and EY (t, p) to obtain the masks MX(t, p) and MY (t, p),
respectively. These masks are combined using the logical OR
operator to produce the final mask M(t, p), i.e., M(t, p) =
MX(t, p)||MY (t, p). This mask, M(t, p), is then applied to
θ(t, p), calculated from EX(t, p) and EY (t, p) based on
Eq.(3), to derive θ1(t, p), serving as the input for the Hist-
WGMM. The DOA estimate, θ, from the Hist-WGMM is
treated as an observation value by the WKF, which yields the
tracking result, θt.

B. Performance of proposed DOA tracking method
A virtual acoustic room with dimensions of 6m × 5m × 4m
is constructed using the image method [18]. The collocated
dipoles and the speaker are placed at (2m,1.5m,1.7m) and
(3.25m,1.5m,1.7m), respectively. And the RT60 and SNR are
set to 0.2s and 15dB, respectively, while the sound speed is
fixed as 340m/s. The speaker is assumed to walk along a
circle with (2m,1.5m,1.7m) as the center point and 1.25m as
the radius. At each time frame, we regard the DOA estimate
obtained by the Hist-WGMM as the observations, while using
WKF and CKF to track the DOAs, respectively. The tracking
result of this simulation is depicted in Fig.5. From the 0th to
150th time frame, the WKF and CKF obtain the same tracking
performance. However, the phase ambiguity problem occurs
around the 160th time frame, where the CKF produces large
oscillations while the proposed WFK gives a stable and precise
result. It is evident that the proposed WKF provides a better
tracking result in the presence of phase ambiguity.
VI. CONCLUSIONS & FUTURE WORK
We propose a DOA tracking method for double collocated
dipole microphones. This method includes two steps: DOA
estimation and the WKF. To estimate DOA, the Hist-WGMM
is proposed to provide fast and robust estimation. We use the
DOA estimated by Hist-WGMM as the input for the pro-
posed WKF. The simulated results demonstrate that the entire
tracking approach can provide accurate results. Additionally,
the proposed Hist-WGMM supports locating multiple sound
sources simultaneously, but the WKF does not support tracking
multiple DOAs simultaneously. Tracking multiple DOAs using
orthogonal dipoles is listed as a focus area for our future work.
