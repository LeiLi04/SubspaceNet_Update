of Multiple Fast-Moving Sources in
Antenna Array based Access Networks
Zhuqing Tang and Athanassios Manikas
Department of Electrical and Electronics Engineering
Imperial College London
Abstract—This paper is concerned with the problem of si-
multaneously tracking the direction-of-arrival (DOA) of multiple
fast moving sources using the vector-signal received by a small
aperture antenna array of N elements. The antenna array may
be located at the Node-B, eNode-B or gNode-B of an access
network and is used in conjunction with a novel trajectory
tracking algorithm. The proposed algorithm incorporates a
"manifold extender" together with a state space model and
an arrayed extended Kalman ﬁlter (EKF). These provide a
spatiotemporal tracking approach of the DOAs and angular
velocities of multiple fast moving sources snapshot-by-snapshot.
Furthermore, two different manifold extenders are employed
in the proposed algorithm and their performance is evaluated
using computer simulation studies and is compared with the
case where the proposed algorithm is implemented without a
"manifold extender".
Index Terms—Array signal processing, source tracking, ex-
tended Kalman ﬁlter, spatiotemporal, direction-of-arrival, array
manifolds, extended manifolds

I. INTRODUCTION
Tracking the direction-of-arrival (DOA) of multiple moving
sources/users1 is a signiﬁcant research topic in array signal
processing, due to its wide applications in sonar, radar, air
trafﬁc control, mobile communications, remote sensing, etc.
For static sources, it is well known that their DOA can be
estimated using, for instance, signal-subspace algorithms (e.g.
1In this paper, the terms "sources", "users", and "emitters" are considered
equivalent and may be used interchangeably.
[1], [2], [3]). If the motion of the sources is slow, then small
time-frames can be used and, for each time-frame, apply a
DOA estimation algorithm (i.e. repetitive DOA estimation)
tracking small variations in DOA.
However, if the source is moving too fast so that the
source cannot be assumed stationary over a small time-
frame, these high-resolution algorithms with repetitive DOA
estimation begin to exhibit performance degradation. Thus,
various iterative DOA algorithms have been proposed which
however suffer from the data association problem. Several
approaches ([4]-[8]) have been proposed to avoid this problem
by maintaining the order of the DOA estimates for different
iterations. However, these approaches suffer from spread array
spatial spectrum effects caused by rapid source motion [9].
To remedy the aforementioned problems, various algorithms
based on state space models have been proposed. For exam-
ple, a maximum likelihood (ML) approach combined with a
multiple target state model has been proposed in [9]. In [10],
a Bayesian model is utilised in conjunction with a particle
ﬁlter to track the targets. However, these approaches have high
computational complexity. In [11], a linear combiner matrix
is constructed as a state variable, which is updated adaptively
by an H-inﬁnity ﬁlter, to obtain the adaptive estimated noise
subspaces for tracking the DOAs.
Another widely adopted family of algorithm for source
tracking is based on the Kalman ﬁlter (KF) which recursively
tracks source parameters snapshot-by-snapshot with the use
of a state space model. It has less computational complexity
and been used in communication systems, e.g. [12]. However,
KF cannot handle nonlinear estimation problems which can
be solved by an extended Kalman ﬁlter (EKF), e.g. [13]-[15].
The proposed algorithm presented in this paper belongs to the
EKF family of algorithm.
In particular, to track the DOA of multiple fast-moving
sources, a novel tracking approach is proposed using a small
aperture antenna array of N elements and a spatiotemporal
state space model integrated with an arrayed EKF algorithm.
This approach utilises a "manifold extender" to increase the
signal observation space from N to NNext (where Next is
a positive integer) and to construct (NNext × 1) extended
manifold vectors. Then, the spatiotemporal state space model
incorporates these extended manifold vectors to track the

integer.
If there is no manifold extender, then Next = 1, and to
increase the dimensionality of the observation space we need
to increase the number of antenna elements N. However, this
implies more hardware resources and consequently, higher
cost.
VI. CONCLUSIONS
In this paper, a novel approach was proposed for tracking
far-ﬁeld fast-moving sources using a small aperture antenna
array. Initially, a spatiotemporal state space model was
presented in conjunction with the concept of the extended
manifold vector. Then, this model was integrated with an
arrayed EKF to provide overall a powerful multi-source
tracking
algorithm.
The
performance
of
the
proposed
approach was examined using computer simulation studies
under various noise levels and the results indicate high
accuracy and robustness to noisy environments.
