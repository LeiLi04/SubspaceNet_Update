nique is proposed to make reliable estimations of the bearing angle of an underwater target with uncertain environment noise.

1. Introduction
Estimating the bearing angle of an uncooperative underwater target is an important research area in the ﬁeld of underwa-
ter acoustic engineering.1–3 Considering the unknown underwater environment sometimes causes uncertain measurement
noise that will degrade the estimation accuracy of the interested target’s bearing angle, a robust bearing angle estimating
technique is necessary and has attracted several researchers’ attention.4,5 In addition, the passive bearing angle estimation
techniques can be divided into two categories, namely, the direction-of-arrival (DOA) estimation that merely depends on
the measurements from a certain period of time and the DOA tracking that depends on both the target’s kinematics and
the measurements of the current tracking step. Although the DOA estimation has already made a lot of milestones in vari-
ous bearing angle estimation missions,6,7 its ignorance of the target’s kinematics makes the DOA estimation techniques
always have superior estimating results when the target is nearly static and the environment is stable. When the target is
maneuvering or the measurement noise shifts since the underwater environment is uncertain, the DOA estimation meth-
ods can hardly have satisfactory results. Also, in order to make reliable and accurate estimations of an uncooperative
underwater target’s bearing angle, the DOA estimation techniques usually need a wide observation window, which will
cost more on-board computational time than the recursively DOA tracking techniques.
Considering the advantages and drawbacks of the DOA estimation methods, DOA tracking techniques have
been proposed. Instead of merely utilizing the measurement information, the DOA tracking methods also consider the tar-
get’s kinematic model as prior information. Based on the Bayesian estimation principle, the DOA tracking techniques can
reach higher estimating accuracy and occupy less on-board computing or storage resources especially when the underwater
target is maneuvering.8,9 Also, the unknown underwater environment sometimes can make the measurement uncertain,
which will degrade the bearing angle estimation accuracy or even make the estimating procedure diverge. Under this
circumstance, the robust DOA tracking technique is necessary to guarantee the accuracy and robustness of the whole esti-
mation process. Among all the robust DOA tracking techniques, two categories can be divided depending on the basic
mathematical principles, namely, the “data-inspiring” robust tracking techniques10 and the Bayesian inference techni-
ques.11,12 As a typical example of the “data-inspiring” robust tracking technique, the Sage-Husa adaptive estimating
method (i.e., Sage-Husa extended Kalman ﬁlter, SH-EKF) has been widely used in a number of studies to prove its efﬁ-
ciency.13–15 The basic concept of the Sage-Husa online estimator is to utilize the good estimations from a long tracking
period to adjust the weight of the bad measurements when the measurement noise becomes uncertain. As a result, the
weights of the poor measurements can become very small to make the current bearing angle estimations mainly depend
on the historically good estimations. Nevertheless, similar to many of the data-driven methods, the Sage-Husa online esti-
mator cannot guarantee its convergence and depends heavily on good historical estimations and measurements. When the

To overcome the drawbacks of the data-inspiring robust tracking techniques, the Bayesian inference techniques,
or the so-called variational Bayesian (VB) robust tracking techniques, are proposed and have been utilized in various
research.16–20 Unlike the data-inspiring robust tracking methods, the VB robust tracking techniques are strictly derived
mathematically. By assuming the prior probability density functions (PDFs) of the tracking parameters and the interested
time-varying parameters (i.e., covariance matrix of the uncertain measurements noise) along with optimizing the Kullback-
Leibler Divergence (KLD) among the posterior PDFs, the VB technique can analytically estimate the tracking parameters
and other interesting parameters simultaneously. Different from the data-inspiring robust tracking methods, the VB robust
tracking techniques have strictly mathematical foundations and can guarantee convergence and accuracy if the prior PDFs
are set properly. Under this circumstance, the VB robust tracking techniques do not need good estimations from a long
period to output reliable results when measurements become uncertain and are more theoretically sophisticated.
Based on the previous analysis, a variational Bayesian based extended Kalman ﬁlter (VB-EKF) is proposed to
make robust and accurate DOA tracking of an underwater target with uncertain measurement noise. First, a uniform cir-
cular array (UCA) that is not only free from the port and starboard ambiguity problem but also possesses a uniform aper-
ture at all bearing angles is considered to provide measurements of the underwater target.13 Then, considering the
unknown underwater disturbance can sometimes make the measurement noise uncertain, the VB-EKF is designed to
simultaneously estimate the bearing angle of the underwater target and the covariance matrix of the uncertain measure-
ment noise. Finally, based on the sea trial data from the South China Sea in July 2021, the proposed VB-EKF is veriﬁed.
The robust and accurate estimation results proved the superior characteristics of the proposed DOA tracking method.

4. Experimental results
4.1 Experimental scenario
The data from an acoustic experiment in the South China Sea in July 2021 are used to verify the superiority of the pro-
posed VB-EKF. An acoustic emission transducer is placed 3 m under the sea surface. A ship equipped with the acoustic
signal emission system and GPS keeps moving when an acoustic signal at 170 Hz is emitted continuously for 7500 s with a
source level of 145 dB.
An underwater buoy system shown in Fig. 2(a) is placed 20 m under the sea surface before the experiment. A
uniform circular hydrophone array with a radius of 1 m and 12 elements is ﬁxed on the underwater buoy along with a
digital acquisition system and GPS. During the acoustic signal emitting, the digital acquisition system sampled the output
of the array at a frequency of 8192 Hz. Before the experiment, the sound velocity is measured to be constant at 1544 m/s
at a depth from 0 to 20 m with a CTD proﬁler. The GPSs record the locations of the acoustic signal emission system and
the underwater buoy system every second. Considering the bearing angle of the target is measured clockwise with respect
to the north, the bearing angle trajectory is calculated by the GPS data.
The tracking scenario is shown in Fig. 2(b), and the longest range between the target and the sonar array
is 5462 m and the shortest range is 3113 m. In addition, the total distance that the target moved during the tracking

In addition, there are many factors in the ocean environment can make the environmental noise varies
uncertainly.23 For example, the turbulence in the form of irregular random ﬂow in the ocean and the pressure inside the
turbulence produce acoustic effects. The formation and extinction of the turbulence will result in the variation of the envi-
ronmental noise. Also, the cavitation or bursting of bubbles and the turbulent pressure caused by sea waves and winds
generate noise. Thus, the variation of sea conditions and wind strength results in variation in environmental noise.
Meanwhile, the radiated noise from distant ships is also an important source of environmental noise. The variation of the
position, sailing status, and number of ships will also cause variation in environmental noise. Such variation of environ-
mental noise results in the variation of statistical characteristics of environmental noise, which will make the covariance
matrix of the measurement noise shift during the DOA tracking period.
Thus, in order to further test the robustness of the proposed VB-EKF to uncertain measurement noise, a period
of correlated Gaussian noise generated by a technique from Ref. 24 is added to the raw measurement data from 2600 to
3000 s, and then the methods are tested again. The results are shown in Figs. 3(c), 3(d), and Table 1. Also, the Euclidean
distance between the estimated covariance matrix and the real covariance matrix of the measurement noise by different
DOA tracking algorithms is shown in Fig. 4.
Figures 3(a) and 3(b) show that the trajectories obtained by all methods using raw experimental data ﬂuctuated
around the true trajectory due to the effect of the underwater environmental noise. Since the MUSIC only depends on the
batch measurements and does not utilize the target’s kinematic information, the trajectory obtained by MUSIC is the most
ﬂuctuant and provided a large error when the raw measurements are in low SNR. The precision of the EKF is higher than
that of the MUSIC since the EKF is based on the DOA tracking principle that not only utilizes the current measurement
but also considers the kinematic model of the underwater target. The trajectory obtained by the robust DOA tracking algo-
rithms, namely, the SH-EKF and the VB-EKF are closest to the true trajectory. The reason is that the robust DOA tracking
algorithms provide more accurate Kalman ﬁlter gains than EKF due to the high-precision estimation of the MNCM, which
can signiﬁcantly improve the precision of the DOA tracking. Also, it can be found that the proposed VB-EKF has higher
DOA tracking accuracy compared to the SH-EKF since the VB-EKF is analytically derived, which makes it a precise math-
ematical model of the unknown MNCM and makes it more accurately estimated. However, the data-inspiring SH-EKF

balanced by engineering experience.
The estimation results of the covariance matrix of the measurement noise are shown in Fig. 4 by ED described
by Eq. (11). During the measurement noise shifting period, both the SH-EKF and VB-EKF have the ability to make esti-
mations of the covariance matrix of the measurement noise when compared to the EKF, which has a ﬁxed one. By esti-
mating the covariance matrix of the measurement noise, the SH-EKF and VB-EKF can make more reliable Kalman gain
that can then deal with the shifting measurement noise. In addition, since the SH-EKF mainly depends on historically
good estimations to output the current DOA tracking result, it can make faster covariance matrix estimations at the begin-
ning of the shifting of the measurement noise. However, when the measurement noise becomes normal, affected by the
historical estimations, the SH-EKF has ﬂuctuations and ﬁnally leads to a lower estimation accuracy than the VB-EKF. On
the contrary, since the VB-EKF is based on the analytical VB derivations, it has a slower covariance matrix estimating
speed during the whole measurement noise shifting period. However, when the measurement noise becomes normal, the
VB-EKF can still provide reliable estimations that ﬁnally make the VB-EKF have the highest DOA tracking accuracy.
5. Conclusion
A VB-EKF robust DOA tracking method is proposed and veriﬁed. The theoretical derivations have been given comprehen-
sively and the performance has been proven by the real sea trial data. The experiment results show the proposed VB-EKF
has more accurate estimation results and is also robust to uncertain underwater disturbance.
Future work should focus on enhancing the computational efﬁciency of the proposed VB-EKF since the parame-
ter iteration steps during the VB procedure have a huge computational load, especially when the measurements are highly
nonlinear. As a result, to make the proposed robust DOA tracking algorithm suitable for real time on-board utilization,
enhancing its efﬁciency is necessary.
Also, the proposed VB-EKF still assumes the uncertain measurement noise to be Gaussian. However, non-
Gaussian measurement noise is more common in real DOA tracking scenarios. To make the robust DOA tracking tech-
nique more accurate, nonlinear and non-Gaussian tracking schemes should be considered to work with the VB technique.
