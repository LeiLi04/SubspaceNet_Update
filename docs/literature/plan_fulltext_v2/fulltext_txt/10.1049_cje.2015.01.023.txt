A Sequential Bayesian Algorithm for DOA
Tracking in Time-Varying Environments∗
GAO Xunzhang1, LI Xiang1, Jason Filos2 and DAI Wei2
(1. College of Electronic Science and Engineering, National University of Defense Technology, Changsha 410073, China)
(2. Department of Electrical and Electronic Engineering, Imperial College London, London SW7 2AZ, United Kingdom)
Abstract — This paper focuses on the Direction of ar-
rival (DOA) tracking problem in dynamic environments
where each source signal is modeled as a Gaussian process
with time-varying mean and unknown covariance. In the
presence of highly dynamic environment, benchmark algo-
rithms usually have deteriorated performance. By treating
the source signals as a function of the arrival angles, a
sequential Bayesian tracking approach named Simultane-
ous angle-source update (SASU) is proposed based on the
Maximum a posteriori (MAP) principle. The key feature of
the proposed approach is to simultaneously update the ar-
rival angles and the source signals in the Kalman ﬁlter step
by converting the update process of the state vector into a
joint optimization problem. An iterative Newton method
to eﬃciently solve the joint optimization problem is pro-
posed. The accuracy and robustness of the proposed SASU
algorithm is demonstrated via simulations.
Key words — Time-varying, DOA tracking, Sequential
Bayesian, Kalman ﬁltering.
I. Introduction
Direction of arrival (DOA) tracking using sensor arrays is
a classical problem in array signal processing due to its wide
applications in radar, sonar and communications.
Two popular approaches for tracking arrival angles have
been proposed in the past decades. The ﬁrst approach is
characterized by a two-step procedure that ﬁrst estimates
the arrival angles and then associates the estimated angles
with the right targets. For DOA estimation, beamforming and
signal-subspace techniques such as Multiple signal classiﬁca-
tion (MUSIC)[1], Estimation of signal parameters via rota-
tional invariance techniques (ESPRIT)[2] as well as maximum
likelihood principle based methods[3,4] are widely used. Due
to the need of multiple snapshots from diﬀerent time in-
stants, these approaches are not suitable for fast moving tar-
gets. Furthermore the extra steps of data association intro-
duce unnecessary computational complexity and performance
loss[5]. The other approach is marked by applying tracking
algorithms, for example, Kalman ﬁltering (KF), directly to
arrival angles[4]. This approach often requires more prior in-
formation, including proper statistical modeling, knowledge
about the number of sources, and reasonable initial estimates
of arrival angles. Since the measurement vector is a nonlinear
function of the arrival angles, nonlinear Kalman ﬁlters, such
as the Extended Kalman ﬁlter (EKF)[6,7], Iterated extended
Kalman ﬁlter (IEKF)[8], Unscented Kalman ﬁlter (UKF)[9]
and Particle ﬁlter (PF)[10] have been proposed.
In these approaches, two diﬀerent signal models are con-
sidered according to diﬀerent applications: the deterministic
model and the stochastic model[11]. The former assumes the
tracked signals to be deterministic but unknown, and the lat-
ter characterizes the tracked signals as a stochastic process. In
practical applications, some signals such as the acoustic sig-
nals from submarines and radar echoes from stealth objects
are often assumed to be complex random process with time-
varying mean and unknown covariance[12,13]. In the presence
of this time-varying source environment, the aforementioned
benchmark algorithms usually fail or have deteriorated perfor-
mance.
The main contribution of this paper is proposing a novel
sequential Bayesian tracking approach named Simultaneous
angle-source update (SASU), which focuses on the dynamic
signal tracking problem discussed in Ref.[12] where each source
signal is modeled as the mixture of a zero-mean Gaussian sig-
nal and a sinusoidal component with unknown coeﬃcients and
frequencies. For simplicity, we assume that the frequencies of
the sinusoids and the covariance of the Gaussian noise have
been estimated previously. Based on the Maximum a poste-
riori (MAP) principle in Bayesian statistics, the update pro-
cess of the state vector is converted into a joint optimization
problem at each discrete time instant during the tracking pe-
riod. The key feature of our proposed approach is to simul-
taneously update the arrival angles and the source signals in
the KF step by treating the source signals as a function of
the arrival angles. With this treatment, the cost function of
the joint optimization problem involves only the arrival an-

Finally, we would like to comment on the complexity of
the proposed algorithm. The computational cost of the SASU
algorithm is related to two factors. The ﬁrst factor is related
to the computation of the inverse matrix in each iteration. To
avoid this problem, we use inverse of the covariance matrix as
an iterative variable by the Woodbury equality as mentioned
before. The other factor is the number of the iterations. Sim-
ulations show that ideal results can be achieved while the it-
erations run no more than 100 times.
V. Conclusion
We have presented a robust DOA tracking algorithm that
simultaneously updates the arrival angles and the source sig-
nals in the KF step and design a iterative Newton method
to solve the joint optimization problem. Experimental results
show the higher tracking accuracy and robustness of the pro-
posed SASU approach when compared to benchmark UKF,
EKF and IEKF algorithms in time-varying environments.
