Abstract—In this paper, we consider directions of arrival
estimation and tracking in a noisy and relatively adverse
scenario. We thus use a robust subspace tracking method
called HTFAPI that exploits a weighted least squares cri-
terion to enhance robustness, followed by a smoothing
Kalman ﬁlter to improve the estimation. Simulated experi-
ments are then presented to show that the proposed solution
enhances the performance when dealing with corrupted
data in an impulsive noise environment.
Index Terms—Robust subspace tracking, Directions of
arrival, Kalman ﬁlter, Impulsive noise
I. INTRODUCTION
Directions of Arrival (DoA) estimation and tracking
have long presented a great interest in signal processing.
In the array signal processing literature, we can come
across several research axes on this subject. The high-
resolution methods that appeared in that context were
the MUltiple SIgnal Classiﬁcation (MUSIC) method [1]
and the Estimation of Signal Parameters via Rotational
Invariance Techniques (ESPRIT) algorithm [2]. However,
these methods are very expensive in terms of complexity
due to the eigendecomposition of the data covariance.
Thankfully, adaptive algorithms with linear complexity
have emerged to lower the cost of this eigendecomposi-
tion step, such as the Projection Approximation Subspace
Tracking (PAST) [3], Orthogonal PAST (OPAST) [4],
PAST with the deﬂation technique (PASTd) [3], Fast
Approximated Power Iteration (FAPI) [5] and Fast Data
Projection Method (FDPM) [6]. Furthermore, to improve
the results of these algorithms, several works have pro-
posed to add a Kalman ﬁlter to smooth the tracking of
the DoA [7]–[9]. Despite their efﬁciency, these algorithms
lack of robustness. Indeed, in the presence of impulsive
noise, these algorithms diverge and the estimation error
of the DoA becomes too large. In this context, one can
ﬁnd several subspace tracking algorithms that deal with
this problem [10]–[13]. It has been shown in [14] that
the algorithm called Mahalanobis FAPI (MFAPI) and its
lower cost version Hard Thresholding FAPI (HTFAPI)
out-perform the other algorithms in the presence of data
corrupted by impulsive noise or outliers.
The latter algorithms propose to replace the least
squares (LS) criterion of the subspace estimation with
a more appropriate weighted criterion WLS “Weighted
LS” in order to mitigate the impact of corrupted data
through the choice of a proper attenuation coefﬁcient as
in [10]. In addition, they use the projection approximation
proposed in [5] which helps to reduce signiﬁcantly the
computational cost.
In this article, we propose to smooth the results ob-
tained by the HTFAPI algorithm by using a Kalman ﬁlter.
More precisely, the directions of arrival given by HTFAPI
followed by ESPRIT algorithms are considered to be
measurements for Kalman’s ﬁlter. Besides, the parame-
ters of the latter are chosen dynamically according to the
weighting coefﬁcient of the algorithm HTFAPI.
The rest of this article is organized as follows. Section
II details the problem statement. Then, the HTFAPI algo-
rithm is introduced in section III. The proposed algorithm
is presented in section IV, while the results of simulations
are given in section V. Finally, section VI concludes this
paper.
