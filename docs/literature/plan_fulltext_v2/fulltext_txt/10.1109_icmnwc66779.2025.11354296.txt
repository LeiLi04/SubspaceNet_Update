Abstract—Accurate direction of arrival (DOA) estimation in
noisy and interference-prone environments remains a critical
challenge in modern radar systems, particularly when targets
are closely spaced. This work introduces a Wigner-Ville Dis-
tribution (WVD) assisted MUSIC algorithm that utilizes the
high-resolution time-frequency characteristics of WVD to en-
hance subspace separation and improve estimation accuracy. By
incorporating WVD-based preprocessing, the proposed method
significantly mitigates the impact of noise and electronic coun-
termeasures, ensuring reliable detection of multiple targets with
minimal angular separation. Extensive simulations using pas-
sive radar configurations demonstrate that the WVD-MUSIC
approach achieves notable improvements in position and velocity
root mean square errors compared to conventional MUSIC
and short-time Fourier transform-based MUSIC techniques. The
results affirm the robustness of the proposed method for tracking
in dynamic and cluttered scenarios, making it a promising
solution for defense and civilian applications requiring precise
DOA estimation under challenging conditions.

I. INTRODUCTION
Radar
systems
have
long
been
the
cornerstone
of
surveillance, navigation, and defense applications, providing
critical information on the location and movement of targets.
A fundamental task in radar signal processing is Direction
of Arrival (DOA) estimation, which refers to determining
the angle at which a signal arrives at an array of sensors.
Accurate estimation of DOA is crucial for applications such
as air traffic control, maritime navigation, electronic warfare,
and emerging technologies such as autonomous vehicles. In
particular, the need to resolve targets with very small angular
separations has become increasingly important due to the
growing density of aerial and surface objects in operational
environments [1].
Active radar systems traditionally have dominated the
field by transmitting their own waveforms and analyzing the
echoes reflected from the targets. However, these systems face
two significant challenges: detectability and susceptibility to
electronic countermeasures (ECM). To overcome these issues,
passive radar systems have gained significant attention in
recent years. Passive radars do not transmit signals; instead,
they exploit existing non-cooperative illumination sources
such as commercial radio, television, or cellular signals to
track targets. This not only makes the radar system covert
and less vulnerable to jamming but also offers economic and
operational benefits by reducing hardware complexity. Despite
these advantages, passive systems operate in interference-
rich environments, and therefore demand advanced signal
processing techniques for robust DOA estimation [2].
Over the years, several algorithms have been developed for
DOA estimation, each with its own advantages and limitations.
Early techniques relied on beamforming methods, which,
while simple, suffer from limited resolution due to array
aperture constraints. To overcome these limitations, subspace-
based algorithms such as Multiple Signal Classification
(MUSIC) and Estimation of Signal Parameters via Rotational
Invariance Techniques (ESPRIT) were introduced. MUSIC, in
particular, has been widely adopted because of its ability to

However, these methods assume stationary signals and often
degrade in performance when targets are closely spaced or in
the presence of strong interference [3], [4].
In recent years, time-frequency analysis techniques have
been integrated into DOA estimation frameworks to handle
non-stationary signals. Methods based on the Short-Time
Fourier Transform (STFT) and Stockwell Transform have
been proposed to improve the resolution of signals with
varying frequency content. While these techniques introduce
adaptability through windowing, they are still constrained by
trade-offs between time and frequency resolution. Moreover,
their performance deteriorates under conditions of high noise,
clutter, or electronic countermeasures, which are common in
real-world radar deployments [5].
Despite significant progress, existing methods face three
major challenges. First, resolving closely spaced targets
remains difficult for algorithms relying on fixed-window
time-frequency transforms, as they cannot adequately capture
fine signal variations without sacrificing resolution. Second,
in ECM-rich and noisy environments, covariance matrix
estimation becomes unreliable, leading to incorrect subspace
separation and poor DOA estimates. Third, most current
approaches do not fully exploit the joint time-frequency
characteristics of signals, which are essential for scenarios
involving maneuvering targets and dynamic clutter [6], [7].
To address these limitations, this work introduces a Wigner-
Ville Distribution (WVD)-assisted MUSIC algorithm. The
WVD offers superior time-frequency localization compared
to conventional transforms, making it highly effective for
analyzing non-stationary signals with overlapping spectra [8].
By integrating WVD-based preprocessing with the MUSIC
framework, the proposed approach enhances subspace or-
thogonality, leading to significant improvements in angular
resolution and robustness against noise and interference. This
methodology is particularly suited for passive radar applica-
tions, where reliance on non-cooperative sources and cluttered
environments makes DOA estimation a challenging task [9].
The key contributions of the work include the proposal
of a novel DOA estimation algorithm that combines the
high-resolution time-frequency analysis of WVD with the
subspace decomposition capabilities of MUSIC. Utilize WVD
preprocessing to improve covariance matrix conditioning and
reduce the impact of strong interference and clutter. Further,
the proposed algorithm achieves better angular discrimination
compared to conventional MUSIC and STFT-based MUSIC,
even in scenarios with minimal angular separation between
targets. Also, the algorithm is validated through simulations
using passive radar configurations, and its effectiveness is
compared against existing techniques in terms of position and
velocity RMSE metrics.

II. TRACKING SCENARIO FORMULATION
The primary objective of this work is to estimate the Direc-
tion of Arrival (DOA) of signals impinging on passive radar
systems operating in an environment influenced by multiple
interference sources and clutter. Unlike active radars, passive
systems utilize non-cooperative sources of illumination, mak-
ing them less detectable and more resilient to conventional
jamming. However, their dependence on ambient signals in-
troduces additional challenges such as multipath effects, envi-
ronmental disturbances, and electronic countermeasure (ECM)
attacks. Figure 1 illustrates the overall architecture considered
in this study, where two passive radars receive signals in the
presence of environmental factors and intentional jamming
sources [10], [11].

Block diagram of the proposed passive radar DOA estimation and

As shown in the figure, multiple direct-path signals from
external emitters (denoted as Direct Signal-I, II, and III) reach
both passive radars. These signals are contaminated by noise,
clutter, and interference originating from environmental con-
ditions and a stand-off jammer located within the operational
range of the radar system. The jammer introduces deceptive
energy into the system, degrading the ability of traditional
DOA algorithms to separate target signals from interference.
Therefore, an advanced approach is necessary to isolate the
target’s angular information while mitigating the impact of
ECM and noise [12], [13].

combination of direct-path signals, target echoes, and additive

Conventional methods like MUSIC or STFT-based MUSIC

The formulated problem, therefore, involves designing a

III. PROPOSED METHODOLOGY
The proposed framework for DOA estimation and tracking
is WVD based MUSIC algorithm. The system employs a bi-
static radar configuration with two passive sensors arranged as
uniform circular arrays (UCAs), each consisting of N = 100
elements and operating in the VHF band. These radars capture
the composite signals comprising direct-path components,
target reflections, and unwanted interference such as Electronic
Countermeasures (ECM), clutter, and false alarms.
For multi-target tracking, the estimated DOAs are processed
through an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) integrated with
Rao-Blackwellized Monte Carlo Data Association (RBM-
CDA). The EKF predicts and updates the target states using
a nonlinear state-space model, while RBMCDA efficiently
resolves data association uncertainties in multi-target scenar-
ios. The overall methodology concludes with performance
evaluation based on position and velocity Root Mean Square
Errors (RMSE), demonstrating the robustness of the WVD-
assisted MUSIC approach against ECM and noise.
The proposed WVD-assisted MUSIC algorithm combines
the strengths of high-resolution time-frequency analysis with
robust subspace-based direction finding to address challenges
in cluttered and noisy environments. By incorporating the
Wigner-Ville Distribution (WVD) as a preprocessing step, the
algorithm achieves superior localization of signal energy in
the time-frequency domain, allowing accurate discrimination
of non-stationary signals even when targets are closely
spaced. Adaptive thresholding further reduces the impact of
jamming and environmental interference, ensuring reliable
covariance matrix estimation. Unlike conventional MUSIC or
STFT-based approaches, this method maintains high angular
resolution under low SNR conditions and in the presence of
electronic countermeasures. Additionally, integration with an

IV. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
A. Presumptions for Case–1
In the first case study, the trajectories considered are adapted
from benchmark scenario 8 of [15]. The two targets are
assumed to move along parallel paths with constant velocity
and without acceleration throughout the observation interval.
Their initial positions are taken as (10×103, −250, 3×103) m
for target–1 and (10 × 103, 250, 3 × 103) m for target–2. The
trajectories are propagated for a total duration of 220 s. Both
passive uniform circular arrays (UCAs) used for reception
are assumed to be located at (40 × 103, 300, 20) m. The
environment is modeled to include electronic countermeasures,
clutter, and false alarms, with a barrage jammer considered in
the side lobes of the radar. The received signal-to-noise ratio
at each snapshot is kept constant at 20 dB. The direction-of-
arrival estimates are obtained using multiple signal classifi-
cation, short time Fourier based MUSIC, and the proposed
Wigner transform-based MUSIC algorithms. These estimated
angles are then passed to the extended Kalman filter (EKF),
which carries out tracking of both targets under the assumed
conditions.
B. Presumptions for Case–2
For the second case study, benchmark scenario 9 of [16]
is considered. Here, the two targets are initialized with a
range of 68 km, velocity of 290 m/s, and altitude of 3.5 km.
The observation time for this trajectory is 215 s. Unlike
Case–1, the targets execute a maneuver: after 100 s, both
undergo a coordinated 2g turn lasting for a period of 20 s.
Two passive UCAs are assumed for reception, positioned at
(60×103, 15×103, 20) m and (30×103, 0, 20) m, respectively.
The environment assumptions remain consistent with Case–1,
including ECM through a barrage jammer and clutter modeled
using a gamma distribution. The direction-of-arrival informa-
tion is extracted using multiple signal classification, short time
Fourier-based MUSIC, and the proposed Wigner transform-
based MUSIC algorithms. However, under these conditions

V. CONCLUSION
The study presented a Wigner-Ville Distribution (WVD)
assisted MUSIC algorithm for direction of arrival estimation in
passive radar systems operating under noisy and interference-
rich environments. By exploiting the superior time-frequency
localization of WVD, the proposed method enhanced subspace
separation and improved covariance conditioning, leading to
more accurate angular estimation and reliable multi-target
tracking. Simulation results confirmed that the algorithm con-
sistently outperforms conventional MUSIC and STFT-based
MUSIC approaches. In benchmark Scenario-I, the proposed
method reduced position RMSE by 91.56% compared to
MUSIC and by 96.16% relative to STFT-based MUSIC for
single target cases. For multiple targets, the improvement in
position RMSE was 27.21% over MUSIC and 28.03% over
STFT MUSIC. Similarly, the velocity RMSE showed signifi-
cant reductions, with nearly 69.26% and 48.91% improvement
over MUSIC for different targets. These findings highlight
the robustness of the WVD-MUSIC approach, making it a
promising solution for passive radar applications that demand
accurate and resilient DOA estimation.
