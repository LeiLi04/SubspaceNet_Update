Abstract—In dynamic scenarios, traditional single-vector 
hydrophone DOA estimation methods suffer from low accuracy. 
In this study, for the first time, the concept of a single-vector 
hydrophone DOA tracking loop is introduced. It employs an 
average intensity estimator or an active average intensity 
estimator as the phase discriminator and utilizes a second-order 
phase-locked loop to dynamically track the incoming direction of 
the signal. For the optimal synthesis of the velocity, the output of 
the phase discriminator in the phase-locked loop is utilized. 
Computer simulation and analysis confirm the effectiveness of 
this approach, revealing accurate DOA tracking and optimal 
velocity channel combining in dynamic environments. 
Keywords—vector hydrophone, direction-of-arrival, phase-
locked loop 
I. INTRODUCTION 
The complexity and variability of the underwater acoustic 
channel 
show 
significant 
challenges 
for 
underwater 
communication and measurement. A single-vector hydrophone 
(SVH) comprises an omnidirectional pressure sensor and 
dipole-directional particle velocity (acceleration) sensors. It 
can simultaneously measure the sound pressure and particle 
velocity at a specific point in the sound field. A single SVH 
can estimate the direction of arrival (DOA) of a sound source 
and provide good spatial directivity. By utilizing the DOA 
estimation from the signal, the pressure and velocity outputs of 
the vector hydrophone can be combined by proper weights to 
achieve spatial gains of 4.8 to 6 dB in an isotropic noise field 
[1]. 
Several options for DOA estimation are found in the 
literature [2, 3], including average intensity estimator, complex 
intensity estimator, and active average intensity estimator. The 
frequency-domain acoustic intensity method, also known as the 
cross-spectrum method, represents the time-domain acoustic 
intensity in the frequency domain. Its advantage lies in the 
ability to estimate the DOA of multiple targets with distinct 
spectral characteristics within a given bandwidth. It performs 
well in estimating the DOA of wideband signals such as ship-
radiated noise. Yin [4] proposed an active average intensity 
estimator algorithm for CDMA underwater communication 
with an SVH. By utilizing the excellent autocorrelation and 
cross-correlation properties of the pseudo-random sequence 
code, the algorithm can simultaneously estimate the DOAs of 
multiple users in the same frequency band, enabling directional 
communication and increasing the number of users. 
The aforementioned SVH DOA estimation methods are 
based on batch processing, where DOA estimation is 
performed in segments and then electronic rotation is utilized 
to form velocity combinations. These static estimation methods 
do not utilize historical data and cannot achieve continuous 
tracking. In practical applications, when the receiving end 
rotates on the water surface or there is relative motion between 
the transmitter and receiver, the DOA of the communication 
target will change over time, which limits the performance of 
DOA estimation methods and results in low accuracy in 
dynamic scenarios. To address this issue, some algorithms 
have been proposed, like Kalman filtering and particle filtering 
[5]. However, these algorithms contain matrix operations and 
are too complicated to be implemented in real time. 
This study proposes an SVH DOA estimation method 
based on a phase-locked loop (PLL). It utilizes an average 
intensity estimator or active average intensity estimator as the 
phase discriminator and employs a second-order PLL to 
dynamically track the incoming direction of the signal. In the 
meantime, the in-phase branch of the phase discriminator 
outputs the optimal combination of velocity channels. This 
method has low computational complexity and can track the 
signal's direction in real time.

III. ALGORITHM 
Unlike the currently open-loop direction estimation 
algorithms, the method proposed in this study is a closed-loop 
estimation algorithm, as shown in Fig. 1. The algorithm 
comprises 
electronic 
rotation, 
cross-correlation, 
phase 
discriminator, loop filter, and numerically controlled oscillator 
(NCO), forming a phase-locked loop. This approach not only 
obtains the estimated azimuth angle 𝜃෠ but also outputs the 
optimized velocity combinations, which are 𝑣௖ and 𝑣௦. 
First, the velocity vector (𝑣௫, 𝑣௬)  undergoes electronic 
rotation, with a rotation angle of 𝜃෠, which is outputted by 
NCO. Then, an open-loop estimation of the error quantity 
(𝜃−𝜃෠)  is performed. The tracking loop in this study is 
compatible 
with 
various 
DOA 
open-loop 
estimation 
algorithms. These open-loop estimation algorithms are utilized 
to estimate (𝜃−𝜃෠), and the estimated value of ൫𝜃−𝜃෠൯ serves 
as the output of the phase discriminator. After passing through 
the loop filter, it controls the NCO to generate 𝜃෠. The phase-
locked loop is a negative feedback loop that iteratively 
approaches 0 for ൫𝜃−𝜃෠൯.

IV. SIMULATION AND ANALYSIS

described in this study and the impact of different parameters, 
we use computer simulations. The simulations employ a phase 
discriminator based on the average intensity estimator. 
The main parameter settings are as follows: sampling 
frequency is 48 kHz, carrier frequency is 12 kHz, signal 
waveform is linear frequency modulation (LFM), signal and 
noise bandwidths are 8 kHz, and simulation durations are 200 
seconds. The signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), defined as 𝑆𝑁𝑅=

The simulations include three scenarios: stationary, circular 
motion, and linear motion. 
A. Scenario 1 
The source and hydrophone remain stationary, with 
azimuth angle θ = 60°. In the simulation, we first set the cross-
correlation integration time 𝑇 =  0.1 s  and vary the loop 
bandwidth 𝐵௅ to values of 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4, and 0.5 Hz. The 
simulation results in different SNRs are used to calculate the 
root mean square error (RMSE) of the azimuth estimation, as 
shown in Fig. 3. Fig. 4 shows the comparison between the 
estimated DOA and the true value of θ. 
In Fig. 3, as the loop bandwidth 𝐵௅ decreases, the RMSE 
decreases, which indicates a higher estimation accuracy of the 
algorithm. When SNR > 0 dB, the RMSEs of the DOA 
tracking loop with different 𝐵௅ values are less than 1°, 
demonstrating high precision in tracking and estimation. 
Fig. 5 displays the RMSEs of the azimuth angle estimation 
with 𝐵௅ = 0.1 Hz versus the cross-correlation integration time, 
including 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 0.8, and 1 s. The figure shows that 
shorter integration times have a slight advantage in 
performance. However, the performance difference among 
different integration times is relatively small. Therefore, for 
subsequent simulations, T = 0.1 s is chosen as the cross-
correlation integration time. 
B. Scenario 2 
The source moves in a uniform circular motion around the 
hydrophone, completing one full circle every 20 s. The 
simulation results are shown in Figs. 6, 7, and 8. Fig. 6 depicts 
the RMSE of the azimuth angle estimation for different SNRs, 
with the cross-correlation integration time 𝑇 =  0.1 s  and 
varying loop bandwidth values of 𝐵௅ = 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4, and 
0.5 Hz. Similar to the results in Scenario 1, smaller loop 
bandwidth (𝐵௅) leads to smaller RMSE, which indicates a 
higher estimation accuracy of the algorithm. When SNR > 0 
dB, the RMSEs of the DOA tracking loop with different 𝐵௅ 
values are less than 1°, also demonstrating high precision. 
In this dynamic scenario, the performance of the tracking 
loop is similar to the static scenario, without significant 
estimation errors caused by the circular motion. This is because 
the second-order phase-locked loop not only eliminates 
constant tracking errors but also mitigates constant-speed 
tracking errors. Therefore, in the dynamic scenario of uniform 
circular motion, the proposed DOA tracking loop exhibits no 
dynamic errors. 
Figs. 8 and Fig. 9 show the comparison between the 
estimated DOA and the true value with SNR = 10 dB or −10 
dB, respectively, demonstrating a high degree of consistency 
between the estimated and true values. Fig. 7 displays the 
tracking errors for different loop bandwidths, which indicates 
that tracking loops with smaller loop bandwidths have the 
advantage of higher tracking accuracy but slower convergence 
speed.

V. CONCLUSION 
This study proposes a DOA estimation method for SVH 
based on a PLL, specifically designed for underwater 
communication in dynamic scenarios. The method utilizes 
algorithms such as the average intensity estimator or the active 
average intensity estimator as the phase discriminator. It 
employs a second-order PLL to dynamically track the direction 
of incoming signals, iteratively approaching zero estimation 
error. When the estimation error approaches 0, the in-phase 
branch of the discriminator is the optimal combination of the 
velocity channel. Computer simulation results demonstrate that 
this method can achieve high-precision tracking of the 
incoming signal direction in various dynamic scenarios. 
Notably, the tracking loop proposed in this study is an 
algorithm framework that can be compatible with various 
open-loop DOA estimation methods. It employs these open-
loop 
estimation 
algorithms 
to 
implement 
the 
phase 
discriminator function within the loop. 
Compared with tracking methods such as Kalman filtering 
and particle filtering, the proposed method has the advantage 
of lower computational complexity, making it more suitable for 
low-power underwater acoustic platforms.
