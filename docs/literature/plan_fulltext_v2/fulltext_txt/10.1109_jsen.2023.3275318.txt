A Fast Variational Bayesian Adaptive Extended
Kalman Filter for Robust Underwater
Direction-of-Arrival Tracking
Boxuan Zhang, Xianghao Hou , Yixin Yang , Member, IEEE, Long Yang ,
and Yong Wang , Member, IEEE
Abstract—Underwater direction-of-arrival (DOA) tracking
using hydrophone array is an important research subject in
passive sonar signal processing. In this study, the robust
underwater DOA tracking with the unknown measurement
noise results from underwater environmental noise is con-
cerned. To deal with the unknown measurement noise, the
existing variational Bayesian adaptive extended Kalman filter
(VB-AEKF) for robust underwater DOA tracking jointly esti-
mates the covariance matrix of unknown measurement noise
and the DOA by using variational Bayesian approach in the
framework of extended Kalman filter. However, the iteration
process of VB approach and the nonlinear measurement model based on hydrophone array signal result in significant
computational complexity of the VB-AEKF for DOA tracking. To better solve the problem of robust DOA tracking with
unknown measurement noise covariance matrix, a novel computationally efficient version of the existing VB-AEKF,
which is named the fast variational Bayesian adaptive extended Kalman filter (FVB-AEKF), is proposed. An equivalent
variational iteration process to estimate the measurement noise covariance matrix is derived. The proposed FVB-AEKF
is identical to the VB-AEKF but reduces the computational complexity of the algorithm. The proposed method is tested
via Monte Carlo simulations of an underwater DOA tracking scenario and an experiment in the South China Sea in July
2021. The results of the simulations and the experiment verified the accuracy and the computational efficiency of the
FVB-AEKF.
Index Terms— Adaptive tracking, Kalman filter, nonlinear measurement model, underwater direction-of-arrival (DOA)
tracking, variational Bayesian approach.

DOA estimation methods [5], [6], [7], [8], [9], [10], [11].
Considering the superior characteristics of DOA tracking
methods, a number of research studies have been carried out.
DOA tracking methods are mostly based on the Bayesian
filter, and recursively estimate the state (bearing angle of
the target) from the array-signal-based measurement (output
signal of the hydrophone array containing the signal emitted
by the target) in real time. To deal with the nonlinear relation
between the bearing angle of the target and the measurement,
i.e., nonlinear measurement model, many tracking techniques
have been proposed based on nonlinear Bayesian filters, such
as extended Kalman filter (EKF) [5], unscented Kalman filter
(UKF) [12], cubature Kalman filter (CKF) [13], and particle
filter (PF) [6]. Among these nonlinear Bayesian filters, the
EKF is both robust and computationally efficient due to its
simple algorithm principle. Thus, the EKF is widely used in
engineering applications.

surement noise is usually assumed to be subject to a zero-mean
Gaussian distribution with a known fixed covariance matrix.

However, this measurement noise covariance matrix (MNCM)
may become unknown and unstable in real underwater tracking
scenarios because of the unknown underwater environmental
noise. The mismatch between the actual unknown and unstable
MNCM and the assumed known fixed MNCM will signifi-
cantly degrade the tracking performance. In order to deal with
unknown and unstable MNCM, a number of adaptive Kalman
filters based on different algorithms are proposed by estimating
the MNCM. Zhang et al. [7] used the Sage-Husa (SH) algo-
rithm [14] to estimate the MNCM and carried out a robust
underwater DOA tracking method by substituting the esti-
mated MNCM into EKF. By estimating the MNCM in a max-
imum likelihood (ML) framework, the expectation maximum
adaptive Kalman filter (EM-AKF) was proposed [15], [16].
Sarkka and Nummenmaa [19], and Hartikainen [20] assumed
the MNCM to be subject to an inverse Wishart distribution
and iteratively estimated the MNCM by using a variational
Bayesian approach [17], [18], namely the variational Bayesian
adaptive Kalman filter (VB-AKF). Huang et al. [21] assumed
the mean square error matrix to be also subject to an inverse
Wishart distribution and jointly estimate it along with MNCM
to improve the performance of VB-AKF. In the existing works,
the superior accuracy and stability of the VB-AKF were
demonstrated [19], [20], [21]. However, due to the iteration
process of the variational Bayesian approach, the VB-AKF has
significant computation for high-dimensional systems. In order
to address this problem, Xu et al. [22] proposed a com-
putationally efficient VB-AKF for a liner transfer alignment
system with a high-dimensional state. In addition, based on the
VB-AKF [19], Hou et al. [23] derived a variational Bayesian
adaptive extended Kalman filter (VB-AEKF) to adaptively
estimate the MNCM for robust underwater DOA tracking.
In this article, robust underwater DOA tracking with regard
to unknown measurement noise was studied. The abovemen-
tioned VB-AEKF for DOA tracking [23] is also proposed
for the same task. Similar to VB-AKF, the VB-AEKF for
DOA tracking uses the variational Bayesian approach in the
framework of the extended Kalman filter to jointly estimate
the state and the covariance of measurement noise, and the
MNCM needs to update by several variational iterations to
ensure the estimation accuracy. It should be noted that the
array-signal-based measurements bring a high-dimensional
and complicated nonlinear measurement model, which leads
to significant computations of the measurement function and
the Jacobian matrix. For such a measurement model, the
variational iteration process of the VB-AEKF results in a
high computational overload. This problem goes against the
application of VB-AEKF for DOA tracking.
To avoid this issue of VB-AEKF for DOA tracking,
a fast VB-AEKF (FVB-AEKF) for DOA tracking is pro-
posed in this article. Inspired by the computationally efficient
VB-AKF [22] for linear systems with high-dimensional states,
we derive a variational iteration process equivalent to that of
the VB-AEKF but more computationally efficient for DOA
tracking with a high-dimensional and nonlinear measurement
model. The proposed variational iteration process degrades
the number of computations by reducing the iterations of
parameters, such as the measurement function and its Jacobian
matrix. Based on this novel variational iteration process, the

FVB-AEKF is proposed. Rich simulations and experiments
are carried out in the underwater DOA tracking scenario with
unknown measurement noise, and the superior performance
of the proposed FVB-AEKF both in terms of robustness and
computational efficiency is verified.

Section II, the underwater DOA tracking problem is formu-

Considering the bearing angle of the target varies in a
wide range in a long-time tracking scenario, the uniform
circular array (UCA) is used since it is free from the port and

B. Verification of VB-AEKF and FVB-AEKF
1) Bearing Angle Estimate Results: The bearing angle track-
ing results of the FVB-AEKF for DOA tracking are shown in
Fig. 2. The results of the CBF, the EKF, the SH-AEKF, and
the VB-AEKF are also shown in Fig. 2 for comparison.
Fig. 2 shows that the performance of the CBF was accept-
able when the measurement noise was weak and station-
ary. However, the CBF was invalid during the strong noise
from 3600 to 4000 s due to its excessive reliance on the present
measurement. The trajectory obtained by the DOA tracking
methods EKF, SH-AEKF, VB-AEKF, and FVB-AEKF fluc-
tuated in the beginning and stabilized after about 1000 s,
which means these algorithms converged after 1000 s. The
time for stabilization cost by these DOA tracking methods is
acceptable in the application. The reason is that some errors
existed in the initial state estimate ˆx0 and the mean square
error matrix P0, which affect the tracking performance at the
beginning. After these methods were performed for a period
of time, the error was gradually eliminated by the correction
of the new measurements. The precision of the EKF was
higher than that of the CBF-based DOA estimation after the
algorithm converged. Furthermore, since EKF considers the
kinematic model of the underwater target, the EKF provided
valid tracking even from 3600 to 4000 s. The SH-AEKF is less
affected by the strong noise because it provides more accurate
Kalman filter gains due to the estimation of the MNCM,
which can significantly improve the tracking performance. The
tracking path of the FVB-AEKF was largely consistent with
the VB-AEKF since the FVB-AEKF is another mathematical
expression of the VB-AEKF. Moreover, the VB-AEKF and the
FVB-AEKF provided the most high-precision tracking. The
reason is that the VB-AEKF and the FVB-AEKF give the prior
distribution of MNCM, and uses the VB iteration method to
jointly estimate the MNCM and the target state, which improve
the accuracy of MNCM estimation.

A. Experimental Setup and Description
The data from an acoustic experiment in the South China
Sea in July 2021 was used to test the FVB-AEKF. An acoustic

circle. Considering the underwater buoy system rotated with
the waves, a compass system was fixed on it as shown in
Fig. 4(b) to measure the bearing of hydrophone 1 to offset
the rotation angle. In Fig. 4(b), the rectangle represents the
compass system, and ϕ (measured clockwise with respect

sampled the output of the circular hydrophone array at a

VII. CONCLUSION
The robust underwater DOA tracking with a nonlinear
measurement model in the scenario of unknown measure-
ment noise is studied in this article. First, for the sake of
accurate tracking and minimum underwater acoustic resource
utilization, the kinematic model of the underwater target by
bearing angle and the nonlinear measurement model based
on the received signal of a UCA are designed. Then, based
on the determined model, the algorithm of VB-AEKF for
robust underwater DOA tracking with unknown measurement
noise is given. Finally, by deriving the equivalent process with
less computations of VB-AEKF, the FVB-AEKF for DOA
tracking with nonlinear and high-dimensional measurement is
proposed. The FVB-AEKF improves computational efficiency
